# SOMSense Background Study and Bug Prevention Baseline

## 1. Purpose and Authority Context

Dr. Fizar Ahmed's ESRC technical calibration pathway (29 Sep 2026) sets one clear rule for soil sensing in Bangladesh. Multi-parameter field probes do not read Soil Organic Matter (`%OM`) directly. Any screen that displays raw probe output as `%OM` misleads growers and regional researchers alike. SOMSense replaces uncalibrated probe readings with calibrated `Estimated SOM (%)` alongside a `95% Prediction Interval`, an active `Model Version`, and an explicit `Calibration Domain Status` (`In-Range`, `Caution`, or `Outside Calibration Range`).

The baseline prediction path estimates Soil Organic Carbon (`%SOC`) using a standardized nine-term linear equation with environmental interactions over `N`, `P`, `K`, `pH`, `EC`, soil temperature (`T`), soil moisture (`M`), `z_T * z_M`, and `z_EC * z_M`, where every mean and standard deviation comes exclusively from training folds. Organic Matter follows the configurable Van Bemmelen multiplier `SOM (%) = k * max(0, SOC (%))` with `k = 1.724` as the default. Active carbon (`POXC` in `mg C/kg dry soil`) runs on a separate modeling track, and the platform blocks any calculation claiming `Passive C = Total SOC - POXC`.

## 2. Prior Art Teardown across Soil-IoT Probes and Public Benchmarks

Low-cost 7-in-1 and 8-in-1 RS485 soil probes, including `ESRC Device - 1` feeding `iot.websoftbd.net`, measure bulk dielectric permittivity for moisture, thermistor temperature, glass or ISFET electrode potential for `pH`, and bulk pore-water electrical conductivity (`EC`). Firmware often derives `N`, `P`, and `K` indexes via proprietary impedance tables calibrated on mineral salt solutions. Organic carbon behaves differently. Humic substances and particulate organic residues stay locked inside solid-phase organo-mineral aggregates, leaving electrical probes sensitive to soil water and dissolved salts while missing solid-phase carbon.

Public datasets in this repository prove both halves of that physical claim. Look at the Kaggle Soil Fertility benchmark (`rahuljaiswalonkaggle/soil-fertility-dataset`, `data/raw/kaggle_soil_fertility/dataset1.csv`). Across 880 total rows measuring `N`, `P`, `K`, `pH`, `EC`, and micronutrients against organic carbon (`OC`), seven rows fail basic soil physics before modeling even begins. Rows 519 and 521 record `pH = 0.90`, rows 531 to 533 climb from `pH = 10.05` to `11.15`, row 659 records `Zn = 42.0` against a clean ceiling of `0.85`, and row 667 logs `OC = 24.0` paired with `Zn = 38.0` where clean `OC` peaks at `1.27`. Drop those seven rows (`n = 873`), standardize the five sensor-like mineral inputs (`N`, `P`, `K`, `pH`, `EC`), and fit a 5-fold Ridge model for `OC`. Cross-validated $R^2$ drops negative (`-0.0803` unshuffled, `-0.0058` shuffled). Five channels fail. Mineral probe readings alone lack soil temperature, moisture, and local Agro-Ecological Zone (AEZ) metadata needed to predict carbon across sites.

Regional laboratory archives carry different pitfalls. The SRDI Kushtia 2024 dataset (`Supplementary_Data_S10.csv`, DOI `10.17632/frkzb57y7p.1`) covers 171 rows across 57 GPS grid points (`Sample_ID` 1 to 57) sampled at three depths (`0-20`, `20-40`, and `40-60 cm`) across Ghior, Gopalpur, Sara, and Ishurdi soil series. Measured `OM_pct` spans `1.2%` to `2.7%` (mean `1.575%`), `pH` runs `6.9` to `8.0`, `Moisture_pct` ranges from `12.0%` to `72.0%`, and exchangeable `K_cmol_kg` sits between `0.07` and `0.46 cmol(+)/kg`. Student pipelines built on this table routinely hit two traps. First, `TN_pct` was computed arithmetically from `OM_pct` (`TN = 0.0492 * OM - 0.0065`, Pearson `r = 0.9776`), so keeping `TN_pct` creates circular leakage with illusory $R^2$ above `0.95` that vanishes the moment a field sensor lacks Kjeldahl nitrogen. Second, random row-wise splitting places `0-20 cm` and `20-40 cm` samples from the same GPS `Sample_ID` into opposite folds. Group splits prevent that leak. Evaluating models with `GroupKFold(groups=Sample_ID)` measures true spatial generalization.

Permanganate-oxidizable carbon (`POXC`) adds a third validation benchmark via the USDA-NRCS Kellogg Soil Survey Laboratory dataset (`10.15482/USDA.ADC/1518679`). It comprises 42 samples from 38 pedons (`Pedon_ID`) tested across 420 combinations of preparation (`< 2 mm Grind` and `< 0.18 mm Grind`) and sample mass (`0.25`, `0.5`, `1.0`, `2.5`, and `5.0 g`). Inside the standard `< 2 mm Grind` at `2.5 g` subset (42 rows), four samples fail quantification (`POXC_mgkg-1 = NaN`): Histosol sample `233952` (`SOC = 288.4 g/kg`) exhausts permanganate completely (`Conc_mM = 0.00`), Mollisol `231915` (`SOC = 97.3 g/kg`) hits `Conc_mM = 0.01`, and low-carbon mineral samples `134599` and `146821` stay at `Conc_mM = 0.18` with no valid replicate count. Worse still, `POXC_DataDictionary.csv` labels the `SOC` column in `Table.S1.KSSL_SelectedSamples.csv` as `% ESTIMATED ORGANIC CARBON`, yet sample `64511` lists `SOC = 28` alongside `< 0.18 mm / 2.5 g` `POXC = 1182.763 mg/kg` and `Ratio_poxc-soc = 0.042` (`1182.763 / 28000 = 0.04224`). Units matter. Those values represent `g C/kg` and require division by `10` to reach `%SOC`.

Remote sensing and standard lab workflows leave wide gaps for smallholder plots. Optical satellite indices from Sentinel-2 lose bare-soil visibility for months under monsoon cloud cover and standing Aman or Boro rice canopies. Conventional Walkley-Black titration at regional laboratories takes seven to fourteen days per batch, leaving extension officers unable to guide basal compost or urea splits before transplanting.

## 3. Rural Bangladesh Field Psychology and Adoption Barriers

Field trials at `cumilla 2` (`AEZ-19`) and Kushtia (`AEZ-11`) reveal physical and cognitive constraints that desktop dashboards ignore. Outdoor phone screens washed out by midday sunlight quickly render faint gray typography unreadable, demanding warm paper-like high contrast (`>= 7:1`) over dark slate ink. Technicians walking wet rice bunds wear mud-stained gloves or carry probes in one hand, so small form buttons below `44x44 px` cause mis-taps and dropped sessions.

Char islands lose signal fast. Connectivity across floodplains shifts between weak 3G and complete dead zones. Any workflow that requires a live cloud API for prediction stalls in the field and teaches technicians to leave the tablet in the office bag. Offline execution inside the browser in under `50 ms` keeps inference synchronous with probe insertion.

Trust collapses fastest when software fakes certainty. During real telemetry logging on `ESRC Device - 1` at `cumilla 2`, the unit recorded `pH = 0.12` alongside `Humidity = 100%`. Those readings point to a disconnected or uncalibrated pH probe (or RS485 decimal shift) sitting in water-saturated soil whose free pore water inflates apparent `EC`. Feed `pH = 0.12` and `Humidity = 100%` silently into a regression model to display `Estimated SOM = 1.9%`, and veteran soil scientists reject the whole system on sight. Worse, an inexperienced field worker might cut organic compost application based on corrupted numbers. Red hardware stops pay off. Blocking faulty probes with clear `SENSOR_FAULT_CRITICAL` cards and amber `SATURATION_DISTURBANCE_WARNING` flags matches how technicians already trust field multimeters.

Institutional adoption in Bangladesh also hinges on familiar agronomic reference scales and local data ownership. Researchers and upazila agriculture officers read soil fertility through Bangladesh Agricultural Research Council (BARC) and SRDI upland organic matter tiers (`Very Low < 1.0%`, `Low 1.0-1.7%`, `Medium 1.7-3.4%`, `High 3.4-5.5%`, `Very High > 5.5%`). Mapping calibrated predictions onto BARC classes bridges statistical intervals (`+/- 95% PI`) and practical field decisions, while keeping lab CSVs locally in browser storage resolves privacy hesitation over uploading geo-tagged plot records to paid servers.

## 4. Historical Soil-IoT Bugs and Preemptive Prevention Matrix

Across the repository hardware logs, public lab tables, and prior ML codebases, fourteen specific failure modes emerged during verification.

1. **B-01: Disconnected or Decimal-Shifted Probe (`pH = 0.12`)**. Logged on `ESRC Device - 1` at `cumilla 2`. Feeding `pH = 0.12` into standardized linear or tree predictors pushes `z_pH` past `-15`, distorting `%SOC` and Mahalanobis scores silently.
2. **B-02: Saturated Soil Cross-Talk (`Humidity = 100%`)**. Logged at `cumilla 2`. Water saturation inflates bulk `EC` and triggers false upward shifts in `EC * Moisture` interaction terms unless blocked or flagged.
3. **B-03: SRDI Circular Target Leakage (`TN_pct`)**. In `Supplementary_Data_S10.csv`, `TN = 0.0492 * OM - 0.0065` (`r = 0.9776`). Including `TN_pct` as a predictor yields fake `R2 > 0.95` and breaks completely on field probes lacking Kjeldahl nitrogen.
4. **B-04: SRDI Potassium Unit Mismatch (`cmol(+)/kg` vs `mg/kg`)**. Kushtia lab data reports `K_cmol_kg` (`0.07-0.46`). Passing raw `0.23` into an ESRC `mg/kg` pipeline (`0.23 * 391 = 89.93 mg/kg`) undercounts potassium by 391x.
5. **B-05: Random Cross-Validation Across Depth Profiles**. Random KFold across the 171 SRDI Kushtia rows splits `0-20`, `20-40`, and `40-60 cm` slices of the same `Sample_ID` across train and test sets, overstating spatial accuracy.
6. **B-06: USDA KSSL `SOC` `10x` Unit Error**. `POXC_DataDictionary.csv` calls `SOC` a percentage, but values such as `28.0` on sample `64511` (`Ratio_poxc-soc = 0.042` at `POXC = 1182.76 mg/kg`) are `g C/kg`. Failing to divide by `10` inflates `%SOC` tenfold.
7. **B-07: Mixed Grind Size, Soil Mass, and Silent Reagent Exhaustion in `POXC`**. On KSSL sample `64511`, `< 0.18 mm` grind yields `1182.76 mg/kg` while `< 2 mm` yields `813.04 mg/kg` (`45%` higher), and sample mass ranges from `0.25 g` (`2216.2 mg/kg`) to `5.0 g` (`664.9 mg/kg`). Meanwhile four `< 2 mm / 2.5 g` rows (`134599`, `146821`, `231915`, `233952`) return `NaN` due to permanganate exhaustion or below-detection change.
8. **B-08: Unfiltered Lab/Kaggle Corrupt Outliers**. Seven of 880 rows in `dataset1.csv` contain impossible field chemistry (`pH = 0.90`, `pH = 10.05-11.15`, `Zn = 42.0`, and `OC = 24.0` with `Zn = 38.0`), skewing training means, variances, and tree splits.
9. **B-09: Electrical Conductivity `1000x` Unit Ambiguity (`uS/cm` vs `dS/m`)**. `ESRC Device - 1` firmware profiles output `uS/cm` (such as `520`) or `dS/m` (`0.52`), while `Salinity` tracks roughly `0.5-0.7 * EC_uS_cm`. Ingesting raw `uS/cm` into a `dS/m` model breaks predictions by three orders of magnitude.
10. **B-10: Invalid Passive Carbon Subtraction**. Reporting `Passive C = Total SOC - POXC` mixes analytical methods with overlapping oxidation pools and confuses fraction mass with permanganate reactivity.
11. **B-11: Pre-Split Scaler and OOD Leakage**. Fitting `StandardScaler`, conformal `q_0.95`, or Mahalanobis covariance across all rows before `GroupKFold` leaks validation distribution bounds and narrows prediction intervals artificially.
12. **B-12: Silent Out-of-Domain Extrapolation**. Applying a Kushtia alluvial clay calibration (`pH 6.9-8.0`, `OM 1.2-2.7%`) to an acidic Sylhet tea-hill soil (`pH 4.3`) produces confident wrong numbers unless fenced by min/max and Mahalanobis $D^2$ thresholds.
13. **B-13: Python vs Browser TypeScript Numeric Parity Drift**. `RandomForestRegressor` casts continuous inputs to `float32` inside `sklearn.tree` prediction, whereas JavaScript uses `float64`. Boundary thresholds or differently standardized interaction terms (`z_T * z_M`) drift past `1e-6` unless serialized explicitly.
14. **B-14: CSV Formula Injection and Prototype Pollution**. Unsanitized `site_id`, `device_id`, or `notes` fields starting with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, `\t`, or `\r` execute macros when opened in Excel, and untrusted JSON containing `__proto__` or `constructor` keys compromises client state.
